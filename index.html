Period-by-period performance

Period Comparison

Daily averages for all days, each month and each Monday–Friday week. Select a row to filter the whole dashboard to that period; the highlighted row is the current selection. Scroll sideways on smaller screens — the period column stays in place.

What each column means
Days
Days in the period with callback data.
Staff
Average staff (FTE) logged into the I&R queue, on days a count was recorded.
Selected
Callers who chose the callback option instead of waiting.
Scheduled
Callbacks the caller went on to schedule.
Accepted
Callbacks the caller accepted when the team dialed back.
Serviced
Callback interactions completed by staff.
Acceptance
Accepted ÷ scheduled, for the period as a whole.
All contacts
Callbacks + voicemails + outbound calls serviced.
Per staff
All interactions ÷ staff in queue, averaged over days with a staff count.
Callback AHT
Average handle time of a callback, in minutes.
Backlog / Longest wait
Callbacks not yet dialed and the oldest callback's wait on the last reported day of the period. Down is better.

          12

          Data notes

          1. Source. Sheet1 of the Queue Pivot Project – Move CCSP Screeners workbook. Sheet2 holds working copies of the same columns and is not used.
          2. How the notes are used. Figures, periods and comparisons come from the numeric columns only. The Notes column is used to explain them: chart markers, tooltips, takeaways, the "What the notes explain" table and the context lists. Each note is given a category (staffing change, queue action, training or meeting, schedule, workload) and a one-line explanation; those are summaries, not workbook data.
          3. Staff names. One note named three staff members; the names were replaced with a count.
          4. Periods. Periods come from the dates alone: calendar months and Monday–Friday weeks. Each period is compared with the one before it; "All days" has no comparison.
          5. Markers. Numbered markers on the daily charts are the four key events in the notes: screeners partly moved, nearly all moved, the after-hours purge, and CCSP's two days on the screening spreadsheet.
          6. Staff counts. Some days have no staff count; those days are left out of staff averages and per-staff figures.
          7. Partial fields. Web intake and overnight voicemail counts start partway through the series, and the latest day has queue fields but no call data.
          8. Handle times. Stored in the workbook as clock time; converted to minutes.
          9. Difference column. The workbook's "Difference between Scheduled and Accepted" equals accepted minus scheduled, so it is negative.
          10. Projections & patterns. The backlog projection is a least-squares line through each business day since the largest one-day drop; "staff to hold flat" divides that trend by callbacks serviced per staff member in the latest full week. Both are planning estimates. The staff-vs-callbacks line is fitted across all days with both counts.
          11. Handle hours per staff. Callbacks × callback handle time + voicemails × voicemail handle time + outbound × outbound handle time, divided by staff in queue, on days with a staff count. The 8-hour line is a reference only.
          12. Calendar days. The workbook's calendar-day wait is rounded on later days; this dashboard shows hours and the recorded business days instead.
